## 1. D: The Central Question & The Line That Makes Marriott Different

> **Central Question:**  
> *"What are five years of your company's statements worth, built from assumptions you can defend?"*

### The Line That Makes Marriott Different: Stated "None" for Floor Plan Debt & Merchandise Inventory
- **In Asbury (ABG):** The model is carried by **Floor Plan Notes Payable**—short-term inventory debt from captive automotive finance arms that finances ~95% of vehicle inventory and is treated as an operating liability in FCFE.
- **In Marriott (MAR):** Floor plan notes and merchandise inventory are **"None"** ($0.0M). Marriott operates an **asset-light lodging franchisor and management fee model**. It carries zero physical merchandise inventory because hotel rooms cannot be stockpiled.
- **What Replaces It:** 
  1. **Reimbursed Expenses Pass-Through:** In 2025, Marriott reported \$19,082M of cost reimbursement revenues and equal reimbursed expenses (hotel employee payroll, reservation systems, marketing funds billed to hotel owners at cost with zero profit margin). True gross profit is generated by its **Net Operating Fee Revenues** (\$5,615M in 2025, a ~21.4% margin on total revenues).
  2. **Negative Book Equity:** Marriott operates with deeply negative shareholders' equity (\$-\$3,842M in 2025) because decades of operating cash flows and borrowing have been funneled into aggressive share repurchases (retiring 3%–5% of diluted shares annually), creating an inverted equity base that balances against \$16.2B in senior notes.

## 7. Organic Growth — Learn on Your Own

1. **What is Organic Growth?**  
   Organic growth measures the underlying expansion of revenue generated by the existing store/property base through increased volume, pricing power, and same-store operational improvements, explicitly excluding revenue added through mergers, business acquisitions, or store consolidation.
2. **How Marriott's MD&A Discloses It:**  
   Marriott reports organic growth primarily through **RevPAR (Revenue Per Available Room)** for comparable systemwide properties (holding the room count constant), paired with **Net Rooms Growth** (organic gross additions minus standard contract terminations).
3. **Why the ABG Video Carried 1.8% When Reported Growth Was 4.7%:**  
   In Asbury's Form 10-K, reported revenue growth of 4.7% included the major acquisition of the Jim Koons Automotive dealership chain. Organic same-store volume growth from dealerships Asbury already owned was only **1.8%**. Using 4.7% would have double-counted acquisition growth as permanent internal compounding.
